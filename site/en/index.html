How it works

Three moves, zero friction

Select

A word, a paragraph, a whole email — in Mail, Safari, Slack, Notes, your IDE… anywhere.

Trigger

Hit the shortcut for the action you want. The panel appears next to your pointer and the result streams in, without stealing focus from your window.

Paste

⏎ replaces the selection with the result and restores your clipboard. Esc cancels, ⌘C just copies.

Install

Ready in two minutes

⬇ Download Claudio 1.0

Notarized build in the works — meanwhile, the app builds with a single command from the source.

  1. Unzip and drag Claudio.app into Applications, then launch it: the mustache shows up in your menu bar.
  2. Create your API key at console.anthropic.com and paste it into Claudio's Settings (it's stored in the Keychain).
  3. On your first shortcut, grant the Accessibility permission — needed to read the selection and paste the result.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost?

Claudio is free and open source (MIT license). You use your own Anthropic API key, billed per use: a short proofread or translation costs about 0.2¢ — even heavy use stays within a few dozen cents a month. And if the app earns its keep, you can buy me a coffee.

Where does my text go?

Straight from your Mac to the Anthropic API, over HTTPS. Claudio has no server, no account, no database: nothing is stored or read by anyone else.

Why the Accessibility permission?

It's the official macOS mechanism for reading selected text in another app and simulating the paste. Without it, Claudio can neither capture your selection nor replace it.

Which apps does it work in?

Anywhere text can be selected: Mail, Notes, Pages, Safari, Chrome, Slack, IDEs… Claudio reads the selection through the Accessibility API and falls back to a simulated copy for apps that don't expose it (your clipboard is restored afterwards).

Can I change the shortcuts or the prompts?

Yes, both. Every action has a reconfigurable shortcut and an editable system prompt in Settings — with a button to restore the original.

Why “Claudio”?

Because an assistant this devoted deserved a first name — and a mustache.
